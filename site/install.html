Install Grimalkin

Everything you type stays on this page. It is never sent anywhere, and secrets are not saved. Closing the tab forgets them.

    Let's set up Grimalkin

    Grimalkin is a memory for Claude that you run yourself. This guide asks a few questions, tells you exactly where to click, and writes your settings for you. Plan on about an hour, most of it in Google's console. Three quick choices first.

    Where will it run?

    How will you sign in to it?

    Only one account, yours, will ever be let in.

    What kind of Google account has your calendar?

    Grimalkin reads your calendar and contacts from Google, whichever way you sign in.

    With a personal Gmail account, Google cuts off Grimalkin's calendar access every 7 days until you re-run one command. This guide shows you how. With a Workspace account that never happens, and you can share a personal calendar into it.

    Create your Railway project

    Your address

    Grimalkin needs a web address that Claude can reach. You'll get one from Railway now and come back to set the rest of Railway up at the end.

    1. On GitHub, open webfeathers/grimalkin and press Fork, then Create fork. That gives you your own copy for Railway to build from.
    2. Sign in at railway.com and press New Project → Deploy from GitHub repo, and pick your fork. Allow Railway access to it if asked.
    3. The first build will fail or crash. That's expected: it has no settings yet.
    4. Click the new service, then Settings → Networking → Generate Domain. Copy the address it shows (something like grimalkin-production-1234.up.railway.app) and paste it below.
    You can use your own domain instead, if you add it in the same Networking panel.

    On your own computer, Grimalkin's address is always the same, so there's nothing to do here.

    http://localhost:8080

    Claude on the web or phone can't reach this address. If you later want that, run this guide again and choose Railway.

    Set up Google

    This lets Grimalkin read your calendar and contacts, and lets you sign in with Google. It is the longest step. Sign in to Google with the account that has your calendar.

    1. Open Google Cloud Console: New project. Name it Grimalkin and press Create. Make sure the new project is selected at the top of the page.
    2. Turn on two APIs. Open each link and press Enable: Google Calendar API and People API.
    3. Open Google Auth Platform (older consoles call it OAuth consent screen) and press Get started. App name: Grimalkin. Support and contact email: yours. For Audience, choose Internal. For Audience, choose External. Then under Audience → Test users, add your own Gmail address.
    4. Under Data Access → Add or remove scopes, tick openid, .../auth/userinfo.email, .../auth/userinfo.profile, and paste these two into Manually add scopes, then Update and Save:
    5. Under Clients → Create client, choose Web application, name it Grimalkin, and add each of these under Authorized redirect URIs:
      Press Create.
    6. Google shows a Client ID and Client secret. Paste both here. (If the window closed, open the client again; the secret is under Client secrets.)

    Something went wrong
    What you seeWhat to do
    extension "vector" is not available in the worker's logsClick the Postgres service → Variables, add EXTENSIONS = vector, and redeploy it, then the worker.
    AUTH_SECRET or ALLOWED_SUBJECT is not setA block didn't save. Open that service's Raw Editor, paste it again, and press Update Variables.
    ENOTFOUND or a database connection errorThe database isn't named Postgres. Rename it, or change Postgres in DATABASE_URL to its name.

    What you seeWhat to do

    Google says redirect_uri_mismatchThe sign-in address from step 4 is missing from your Google client, or has a typo.
